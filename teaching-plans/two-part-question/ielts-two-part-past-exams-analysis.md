## 0. How to analyse any Two-Part question (teacher script)

Spend **90 seconds** every time:

| Step | Question to ask | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the **topic background**? | 1 paraphrase line |
| 2 | What is **Q1 task type**? | reasons / problems / effects / ways / factors |
| 3 | What is **Q2 task type**? | solutions / positive-negative / should / realistic? / outweigh |
| 4 | Are Q1 and Q2 **the same type**? | If no, do not mix paragraph jobs |
| 5 | Who is affected? | individuals / society / government / children… |
| 6 | What must appear in conclusion? | one line for Q1 + one line for Q2 |

**Band 7 balance rule:** Q1 body ≈ Q2 body (word count difference ideally ≤ 20%).

## 2. Full Analyses

### Exam 1 — Happiness  
**Prompt**  
*Happiness is considered very important in life. Why is it difficult to define? What factors are important in achieving happiness?*

**Task split**
- Q1: **reasons** (definition difficulty)  
- Q2: **factors** (not solutions; not advantages)

**Trap analysis**
- Students write “how to be happy” as tips only, ignoring **why definition is hard**.  
- Q2 becomes a lifestyle advice list with no ranking.  
- Philosophical over-writing wastes time.

**Band 7 plan**
| Para | Content |
|---|---|
| Intro | Happiness is widely valued, but hard to define; this essay explains why and key factors |
| BP1 Q1 | Subjective meaning; culture/age differences; changes across life stages |
| BP2 Q2 | Health + relationships + purposeful work/security (choose 2–3, develop) |
| Conc | Hard to define because personal/cultural; main factors: … |

**Lexis:** *subjective, wellbeing, life stage, sense of purpose, financial security, interpersonal relationships*  
**Grammar:** *One reason why… is that… / What matters most is…*

**6.5 → 7 upgrade**
- 6.5: *Happiness means different things.*  
- 7.0: *Happiness is difficult to define because it is highly subjective: for some it means emotional calm, while for others it means achievement or social status.*

**Teaching note:** Force students to answer Q1 with **definition difficulty**, not “people are unhappy”.

### Exam 2 — Job satisfaction (Cambridge IELTS 7)  
**Prompt**  
*As most people spend a major part of their adult life at work, job satisfaction is an important element of individual wellbeing. What factors contribute to job satisfaction? How realistic is the expectation of job satisfaction for all workers?*

**Task split**
- Q1: **factors**  
- Q2: **evaluation / realistic?** (opinion-like judgement)

**Trap analysis**
- Q2 ignored or answered with “yes everyone can be happy at work”.  
- Q1 lists 6 factors with no depth.  
- No link between Q1 factors and Q2 realism.

**Band 7 plan**
| Para | Content |
|---|---|
| BP1 | Meaningful work, fair pay, relationships/management, growth chances |
| BP2 | Not fully realistic for all: labour market limits, routine jobs, economic pressure; but can be improved for many |
| Conc | Key factors…; full satisfaction for *all* workers is unlikely, though partial satisfaction is achievable |

**Lexis:** *wellbeing, promotion prospects, work-life balance, job security, intrinsic motivation*  
**Grammar:** *While X contributes to…, it is unrealistic to expect…*

**Analysis insight:** Q2 is the Band 7 discriminator. Strong answers qualify: *possible for many, unrealistic for all*.

### Exam 3 — Technology & relationships  
**Prompt**  
*Nowadays the way many people interact with each other has changed because of technology. In what ways has technology affected the types of relationships people make? Has this become a positive or negative development?*

**Task split**
- Q1: **ways / effects on relationship types**  
- Q2: **positive/negative judgement**

**Trap analysis**
- Q1 becomes generic “technology is good/bad”.  
- Missing “types of relationships” (online friends, long-distance family, weaker local ties).  
- Q2 sits on the fence with no overall view.

**Band 7 plan**
| Q1 points | more long-distance maintenance; broader weak ties via social media; sometimes less face-to-face depth |
| Q2 stance | e.g. *largely positive if balanced* OR *largely negative for community closeness* |
| Support | one clear reason + example + short concession |

**Lexis:** *interact, maintain contact, weak ties, face-to-face interaction, sense of belonging*  
**Must-have sentence for Q2:** *Overall, I would argue that this is largely a … development because…*

### Exam 4 — Foreign films vs local films  
**Prompt**  
*Many people prefer to watch foreign films rather than locally produced films. Why do you think this is the case? Should governments give financial support to local film industries?*

**Task split**
- Q1: **reasons**  
- Q2: **should** (policy opinion)

**Trap analysis**
- Q2 answered as pure culture essay without clear *should/should not*.  
- Extreme answers (“local films will die”) without mechanism.  
- No conditions on funding.

**Band 7 plan**
| Q1 | higher budgets/effects; global stars & marketing; language/international appeal |
| Q2 | Conditional yes: protect cultural identity + quality incentives; avoid blank subsidies |
| Conc | Prefer foreign due to…; governments should support selectively |

**Lexis:** *box-office, subsidies, cultural identity, production values, film industry*  
**Analysis insight:** Best Band 7 answers use **conditional policy** (*should, but only if…*).

### Exam 5 — Advertising everywhere  
**Prompt**  
*There is a lot of advertising today. Why is there so much advertising? Do you think it is a positive or negative development?*

**Task split**
- Q1: **reasons**  
- Q2: **positive/negative**

**Trap analysis**
- Students only complain about ads (Q2) and skip commercial reasons (Q1).  
- No distinction between informative advertising and manipulative advertising.

**Band 7 plan**
| Q1 | business competition; digital platforms make ads cheap/targeted; consumer culture |
| Q2 | largely negative if it encourages overconsumption; short concession: useful product information |
| Lexis | *consumerism, targeted advertising, persuasive techniques, overconsumption, commercial pressure*

---

### Exam 6 — Print vs online reading  
**Prompt**  
*In the future, nobody will buy printed newspapers or books because they will be able to read everything they want online. Why do some people think this? Is this a positive or negative development?*

**Task split**
- Q1: **reasons for that prediction/belief**  
- Q2: **positive/negative**

**Trap analysis**
- Writing only about “I love paper books”.  
- Ignoring cost, convenience, environment, access arguments for Q1.

**Band 7 plan**
| Q1 | cheaper access; convenience/portability; instant updates; less storage |
| Q2 | choose a side: e.g. mixed but concerning for deep reading / publishers OR positive for access |
| Key | Answer *why people think print will disappear*, not only whether you agree it will |

### Exam 7 — Owning a home (Cambridge IELTS 16)  
**Prompt**  
*In some countries, owning a home rather than renting one is very important for people. Why might this be the case? Do you think this is a positive or negative situation?*

**Task split**
- Q1: **reasons**  
- Q2: **positive/negative situation**

**Trap analysis**
- Turning it into “how to buy a house”.  
- Q2 discusses only rich vs poor without clear overall judgement.

**Band 7 plan**
| Q1 | financial security/asset; social status; stability for family; cultural expectation |
| Q2 | e.g. negative overall if it creates debt pressure & inequality; or positive for stability with concession |
| Lexis | *mortgage, asset, financial security, housing market, social status, long-term investment*

**Analysis insight:** Strong essays connect Q1 motives to Q2 social consequences (debt, delayed marriage, inequality).

### Exam 9 — Social media popularity  
**Prompt**  
*In many countries, people are now spending more time using social media. Why is this happening? Is this a positive or negative development?*

**Task split**
- Q1: **reasons**  
- Q2: **positive/negative**

**Trap analysis**
- Only teenage addiction narrative.  
- No adult/work networking angle.  
- Fence-sitting on Q2.

**Band 7 plan**
| Q1 | smartphones + cheap data; need for connection/entertainment; marketing algorithms |
| Q2 | clear stance with one main line (e.g. negative for mental focus; positive for business networking) |
| Lexis | *algorithm, screen time, online community, dopamine loop → prefer: attention span, social connection, digital wellbeing*

### Exam 10 — Weight & fitness (Cambridge IELTS 8 style)  
**Prompt**  
*In some countries the average weight of people is increasing and their levels of health and fitness are decreasing. What do you think are the causes of these problems and what measures could be taken to solve them?*

**Task split**
- Q1: **causes**  
- Q2: **measures/solutions**

**Note:** This is two-part *and* classic cause–solution. Teach overlap with problem-solution lessons.

**Band 7 matching**
| Cause | Measure |
|---|---|
| Sedentary lifestyle | PE requirements / active city design |
| Ultra-processed diets | labelling + limits on junk-food marketing |
| Lack of health education | school & public campaigns |

### Exam 11 — Ageing population  
**Prompt**  
*In many countries, people are living longer than ever before. What problems does this cause for individuals and society? What can individuals and governments do to deal with these problems?*

**Task split**
- Q1: **problems** (must cover **individuals AND society** if asked)  
- Q2: **solutions by individuals AND governments**

**Trap analysis**
- Only society-level pension talk; ignore individuals.  
- Q2 only “government should care”.

**Coverage checklist**
- [ ] Individual problem (e.g. care costs, loneliness, health)  
- [ ] Society problem (pensions, healthcare systems)  
- [ ] Individual action  
- [ ] Government action

### Exam 12 — Too many choices  
**Prompt**  
*Some people believe that nowadays we have too many choices. To what extent do you think this is true? / Why is this happening? Is it a positive or negative development?*  
*(Teaching version used in class)*  
*Nowadays people have more choices than ever before in areas such as products, education and careers. Why is this happening? Is this a positive or negative development?*

**Task split**
- Q1: **reasons**  
- Q2: **positive/negative**

**Band 7 plan**
| Q1 | globalisation; online retail; expanded education/career pathways |
| Q2 | positive for freedom/opportunity, but anxiety/decision fatigue as concession; overall stance required |

### Exam 13 — Working from home  
**Prompt**  
*An increasing number of people are working from home rather than in an office. Why is this happening? What effects does this have on individuals and society?*

**Task split**
- Q1: **reasons**  
- Q2: **effects** (not opinion; not solutions)

**Trap analysis**
- Writing advantages/disadvantages as if asked outweigh.  
- Missing society-level effects (cities, transport, local business).

**Band 7 plan**
| Q1 | technology/platforms; company cost-saving; work-life preference; post-pandemic norms |
| Q2 | individuals: flexibility vs isolation; society: less commuting pollution, pressure on city centres/cafés |
| Lexis | *remote work, productivity, social isolation, urban centres, digital infrastructure*

**Analysis insight:** Q2 = **effects**, so avoid “governments should…”.

### Exam 14 — Museums / attractions & free entry  
**Prompt**  
*Many museums and public attractions charge an entrance fee. Why do they do this? Should they be free for everyone?*

**Task split**
- Q1: **reasons**  
- Q2: **should** (policy)

**Band 7 plan**
| Q1 | maintenance costs; staff; special exhibitions funding |
| Q2 | e.g. free for residents/students + paid for tourists; or free basic access + paid special shows |
| Analysis | Conditional policy answers score better than absolute free/not free.

## 3. Pattern Analysis (what repeats in old exams)

### Pattern A — Reasons + Positive/Negative (most common recently)
Exams 3,5,6,7,8,9,12  
**Skill focus:** clear overall judgement + one concession max.

### Pattern B — Reasons + Should (policy)
Exams 4,14  
**Skill focus:** conditional recommendations.

### Pattern C — Problems/Causes + Solutions/Measures
Exams 10,11  
**Skill focus:** matching solutions to causes.

### Pattern D — Factors + Evaluation
Exams 1 (factors), 2 (realistic?)  
**Skill focus:** abstract explanation without slogan advice.

### Pattern E — Reasons + Effects
Exam 13  
**Skill focus:** do not drift into opinion/solution language.

## 4. Cross-exam Band 7 vs 6.5 comparison

| Feature | Band 6.5 | Band 7–7.5 |
|---|---|---|
| Question coverage | One question thin | Both fully developed |
| Paragraphing | Mixed Q1/Q2 in one paragraph | One question per body paragraph |
| Second-question type | Wrong task (e.g. solutions when asked effects) | Exact task match |
| Opinion Q2 | “Both good and bad” only | Overall stance + support |
| Examples | Vague (*people use phones*) | Specific but concise mechanism |
| Conclusion | Repeats intro OR new idea | One line per question |

## 7. Mini model outlines (not full essays)

### Outline — Exam 7 (home ownership)
- **Intro:** In some cultures home ownership is a major life goal; this essay examines why and whether this is positive.  
- **Q1:** security/asset; status; family stability.  
- **Q2:** Often negative overall because of debt and inequality, despite stability benefits.  
- **Conc:** Ownership valued for security; as a social situation it can be harmful when prices are inflated.

### Outline — Exam 2 (job satisfaction)
- **Q1:** pay fairness; meaningful tasks; growth; respectful management.  
- **Q2:** Unrealistic for *all* workers due to economic structure, but realistic to improve satisfaction for many through better management and training.  

### Outline — Exam 13 (WFH)
- **Q1:** digital tools; employer savings; employee flexibility.  
- **Q2 effects:** individuals gain autonomy but risk isolation; society sees less congestion but weaker city-centre economies.
