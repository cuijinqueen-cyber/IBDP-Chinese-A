2. Why Discussion Essays Cap Many Students at 6.5

In Discuss both views and give your own opinion, Band 6.5 writers often:

ProblemWhat it looks likeBand 7 fix
One-sided discussionOnly develops the preferred viewBoth views get a full paragraph
Hidden / late opinionOpinion appears only in the conclusionOpinion is clear from the introduction and stays consistent
List-style ideasBullet points turned into short sentencesClaim → Explain → Example → Link
Mechanical balance“Some people… Other people…” with thin supportNatural contrast + deeper explanation
Weak conclusionRepeats intro word-for-wordSummarises both sides + restates reasoned opinion

Teaching priority for this student:

  1. Both-views coverage + clear personal stance (Task Response)
  2. Paragraph logic and natural contrast language (CC)
  3. Grammar accuracy in complex contrast/concession sentences (GRA)
  4. Topic vocabulary without forced rare words (LR)

4. Recommended Band-7 Structure (Teach Explicitly)

ParagraphJobMini-formula
IntroductionParaphrase both views + clear opinionWhile A has value, I believe B is more important because…
Body 1View A (not necessarily your view)Claim → Explain → Example → Link
Body 2View B (your view, developed more strongly)Claim → Explain → Example → Link / short rebuttal
ConclusionAcknowledge both + restate opinionNo new ideas

Position options for discussion essays

  • Prefer View B, but accept View A has limited value (most Band-7 friendly)
  • Prefer View A, with conditions
  • Balanced with a clear hierarchy (not “I agree with both equally” with no decision)

5. Detailed Lesson Plans

Lesson 1 — Diagnostic & Target Setting (90 min)

Aim: Establish exact band profile on a real discussion prompt.

Materials:

Some people say that in the digital age, theatres and cinemas are no longer important… Others argue that theatres and cinemas are still important both economically and culturally. Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.

Procedure

  1. Warm-up (10 min)

Ask: In a both-views essay, where should your opinion appear? Correct the myth that opinion only belongs in the conclusion.

  1. Timed diagnostic (40 min)

Full Task 2, exam conditions, 250+ words.

  1. Immediate self-check (15 min)

Student marks:

  • Both views discussed
  • My opinion is clear in the introduction
  • Each body paragraph has one central idea
  • Conclusion matches my opinion
  1. Quick oral feedback (25 min)

Identify top 3 issues only (e.g. thin second view / late opinion / listing).

Homework

  • Rewrite introduction + conclusion only.
  • Start Error Log: Error → Correct form → My rule.

Lesson 2 — Discussion Essay Architecture (90 min)

Aim: Install a reliable Band-7 system using the uploaded mini-lesson.

Materials: Competition vs co-operation planning notes.

Core prompt from material

Some people think that a sense of competition in children should be encouraged. Others believe that children who are taught to co-operate rather than compete become more useful adults. Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.

Procedure

  1. Question decoding (15 min)

Highlight:

  • View 1: encourage competition
  • View 2: teach co-operation → more useful adults
  • Task: discuss both + give your opinion
  1. Structure modelling from the material (20 min)

Upgrade the material’s outline to Band 7:

Material outlineBand 7 version
Intro: benefits of both, co-operation more importantParaphrase debate + state that co-operation should take priority
Why encourage competitionFull paragraph: motivation, resilience, preparation for adult competition
Why teach co-operation (my view)Stronger paragraph: workplace teamwork, collaboration > winning alone
Conclusion: accept both, co-operation betterBrief acknowledgement + clear preference
  1. Idea quality upgrade (25 min)

Turn material bullets into developed points:

6.5 idea (from material)Band 7 upgrade
motivation to work hardercompetitive goals can raise effort and personal standards
self confidencesuccess in contests can build confidence, if managed well
job interviewsadult life includes competitive selection processes
teams / follow boss’s instructionsmost workplaces reward collaboration more than individual victory
collaboration more useful than winningcomplex problems require shared skills, not solo winners
  1. Guided Body 1 writing (25 min)

Student writes the competition paragraph only (12–14 min), then hot correction for development quality.

  1. Wrap-up (5 min)

Student repeats the 4-paragraph formula from memory.

In-class exercises: A1 sort linkers · A3 replace mechanical contrast phrases · A4 cohesive paragraph

Homework

  • Full 40-minute essay on competition vs co-operation.
  • Plus A2 linker choice.

Lesson 3 — Deep Dive: Competition / Co-operation Essay (90 min)

Aim: Raise Task Response by balancing both views and strengthening the preferred side.

Procedure

  1. Homework feedback (20 min)

Score TR/CC first. Check whether Body 2 (co-operation) is clearly stronger.

  1. Opinion clarity clinic (15 min)

Compare:

  • Weak: Both are important.
  • Strong: Although competition can motivate children, I believe co-operation is more valuable in preparing them for adult life.
  1. Planning board (20 min)

Rebuild plan with timing:

  • 5 min plan
  • Body 1 = View A
  • Body 2 = View B + opinion support
  • Optional one-sentence concession in Body 2
  1. Timed rewrite of Body 2 (25 min)

Focus: explanation depth, workplace example, link back to “more useful adults”.

  1. Conclusion drill (10 min)

3–4 sentences: acknowledge competition’s value → restate co-operation preference.

In-class exercises: B1 collocations · B2 vocabulary upgrade · A5 both-views linkers

Homework

  • Complete polished full essay.
  • B3 word forms + B4 phrase-bank sentences.

Lesson 4 — Lexis & Grammar Lift to Band 7 (90 min)

Aim: Reduce recurring errors and upgrade discussion-essay language.

Procedure

  1. Error Log review (20 min)

Typical 6.5 issues on this topic:

  • childrens / childs
  • cooperate / co-operate consistency
  • compete each other → compete with each other
  • article errors: in workplace → in the workplace
  • although… but
  1. Precision vocabulary (25 min)

Build banks for:

  • competition: competitive spirit, drive, outperform, selection process
  • co-operation: collaboration, teamwork, collective goals, interpersonal skills
  • education outcomes: well-rounded adults, transferable skills, long-term usefulness
  1. Complex sentence control (25 min)

Practise only 3 patterns:

  • Contrast: While competition can…, co-operation…
  • Concession: Although…, …
  • Result: As a result / Therefore…
  1. Targeted rewrite (20 min)

Upgrade weakest paragraph with 2 new collocations + 1 concession sentence.

In-class exercises: D1 articles · D2 sentence combining · D3 error correction · E1 paragraph upgrade

Homework

  • D4 conditionals/concession · D6 relative clauses
  • Reuse 5 new items in a 4-sentence mini-paragraph

Lesson 5 — Transfer Topic: Theatres & Cinemas (90 min)

Aim: Transfer the discussion system to the Cambridge digital-age prompt.

Materials: Theatres/cinemas vs online entertainment prompt.

Strong opinion options

  • Online access is convenient, but theatres/cinemas remain important economically and culturally.
  • Online entertainment dominates daily habits, yet live/shared cultural spaces still matter.
  • Avoid “both equal” with no hierarchy.

Procedure

  1. Question decoding (15 min)

View 1: digital age → theatres/cinemas no longer important

View 2: still important economically + culturally

Must cover economic and cultural angles in View 2.

  1. 5-minute plan race (15 min)

Student produces full plan under time pressure.

  1. Model planning board (15 min)
  • BP1: online convenience, choice, cost, accessibility
  • BP2: jobs/tourism/local economy + cultural experience / shared identity / artistic value
  • Opinion: still important, though roles have changed
  1. Timed body writing (35 min)

Write both body paragraphs (30 min). Monitor for missing “economic/cultural” coverage.

  1. Quick check (10 min)

Checklist against Band 7 TR requirements.

In-class exercises: C1–C3 topic vocabulary for digital culture / arts economy

Homework

  • Full timed essay (40 min) on theatres/cinemas.
  • C4 gap-fill paragraph.

Lesson 6 — Precision Rewrite & Cohesion Polish (90 min)

Aim: Turn the theatres/cinemas essay into a stable Band-7 product.

High-frequency 6.5 problems on this topic

  • Discusses “fun” but ignores economic/cultural points
  • Overuses Firstly / On the other hand / In conclusion
  • Vague examples (people like Netflix)
  • Opinion unclear until the end

Procedure

  1. Feedback conference (20 min)

Criterion-by-criterion notes; show one teacher model paragraph.

  1. Cohesion rewrite (20 min)

Replace mechanical linkers; improve paragraph openings.

  1. Example upgrade (15 min)

From: People watch movies online.

To: Streaming platforms offer cheap, immediate access to films at home, which reduces the need for frequent cinema visits.

  1. Full rewrite under time (30 min)

35–40 minutes for complete essay.

  1. Self-mark (5 min)

Use discussion-essay checklist.

In-class exercises: C5 avoid repetition · D5 tense/agreement · E2 theatres paragraph upgrade

Homework

  • One new both-views essay (teacher-selected topic).
  • Review missed grammar from D1–D3.

8. Band 7 Checklist — Discussion Essays

Before every essay

  • I discussed both views
  • My opinion is in the introduction
  • Body 1 = one view, fully developed
  • Body 2 = other view + stronger support for my opinion
  • Conclusion matches introduction
  • No new idea in conclusion

Language control

  • Contrast/concession sentences are accurate
  • Linkers are varied and not mechanical
  • I used 4–6 topic collocations correctly
  • Checked articles, agreement, and countable nouns

A. Linking Words & Cohesion (Discussion Essays)

A1. Sort the linkers (5 min)

Put each linker into the correct group.

however · while · for instance · in contrast · as a result · although · on the other hand · therefore · for example · whereas · nevertheless · specifically · furthermore · despite this · in addition

PurposeLinkers
Add an idea
Show contrast / concession
Show result
Give an example

Answers — A1

  • Add: furthermore, in addition
  • Contrast/concession: however, while, in contrast, although, on the other hand, whereas, nevertheless, despite this
  • Result: as a result, therefore
  • Example: for instance, for example, specifically

A2. Choose the best linker (10 min)

  1. Competition can motivate children. (However / Therefore / For example), it may also create unnecessary stress.
  2. Co-operation prepares young people for teamwork. (Whereas / As a result / For instance), they often adapt better in the workplace.
  3. Online platforms are convenient. (Although / Therefore / In addition) theatres are less convenient, many people still value the live atmosphere.
  4. Streaming is cheap and flexible. (In contrast / Furthermore / Specifically), cinemas create shared cultural experiences.
  5. Some students enjoy competing. (Despite this / For example / Consequently), collaboration remains more useful in most careers.

Answers — A2

  1. However
  2. As a result
  3. Although
  4. In contrast
  5. Despite this

A3. Replace mechanical discussion phrases (10 min)

Rewrite without using: Firstly, Secondly, On the one hand, On the other hand, In conclusion.

  1. On the one hand, competition makes children work harder.
  2. On the other hand, co-operation is more useful for adult life.
  3. Firstly, online entertainment is cheaper.
  4. Secondly, theatres still support local jobs.
  5. In conclusion, both views are important but co-operation is better.

Model answers — A3

  1. One argument in favour of competition is that it can make children work harder.
  2. A stronger point is that co-operation is more useful for adult life.
  3. A common argument is that online entertainment is cheaper.
  4. Equally important, theatres still support local jobs.
  5. Overall, both views have value, but co-operation should take priority.

A4. Build a cohesive paragraph (15 min)

Use at least 4 linkers from the box. Do not start every sentence with a linker.

although · as a result · for instance · however · in addition · therefore

Prompt: Write one body paragraph (90–120 words) explaining why co-operation should be taught to children.

Teacher check

  • Linkers accurate
  • Not every sentence starts with a linker
  • Ideas explained, not listed
  • Clear central idea

  4. Streaming platforms reduce ticket costs. __________, they cannot fully replace live performance.

B5. Gap-fill paragraph — competition vs co-operation (10 min)

although · collaboration · priority · competitive · teamwork · useful · motivation · interpersonal

__________ competition can increase children’s __________, I believe co-operation should take __________. In adult life, most workplaces value __________ and __________ skills more than individual victory. For example, employees are often assessed on __________ rather than on who beats colleagues. As a result, teaching children to work with others is more __________ than focusing only on a __________ mindset.

Answers — B5

Although competition can increase children’s motivation, I believe co-operation should take priority. In adult life, most workplaces value teamwork and interpersonal skills more than individual victory. For example, employees are often assessed on collaboration rather than on who beats colleagues. As a result, teaching children to work with others is more useful than focusing only on a competitive mindset.

D2. Complex sentences — combine (12 min)

Combine using the word in brackets.

  1. Competition can motivate children. It may also cause stress. (although)
  2. Children learn to co-operate. They become more useful at work. (if)
  3. Streaming is convenient. Many people still value live performance. (while)
  4. Theatres create jobs. They also support cultural life. (which)
  5. Online platforms are cheaper. They cannot replace the cinema atmosphere. (even though)
  6. Schools encourage only winning. Pupils may struggle in teams later. (when)

Model answers — D2

  1. Although competition can motivate children, it may also cause stress.
  2. If children learn to co-operate, they become more useful at work.
  3. While streaming is convenient, many people still value live performance.
  4. Theatres create jobs, which also supports / and they also support cultural life.

Best: Theatres create jobs, which helps local economies, and they also support cultural life.

Simple accurate: Theatres create jobs and support cultural life, which makes them socially valuable.

  1. Even though online platforms are cheaper, they cannot replace the cinema atmosphere.

E. Mixed “6.5 → 7” Paragraph Upgrades

E1. Upgrade this discussion paragraph (15 min)

Band 6.5 version

On the one hand, competition is good. Children want to be better than others. It gives confidence. Also, they need it for job interviews. So schools should encourage it.

Tasks

  1. Remove mechanical openers.
  2. Add explanation.
  3. Upgrade 4 vocabulary items.
  4. Use 2 complex sentences.

Model Band 7 version — E1

One reason to encourage competition is that it can raise children’s motivation. When pupils try to outperform their classmates, they often work harder and make faster progress. Success in contests may also build self-confidence. In addition, because adult life includes competitive situations such as job interviews, early experience of competition can help young people prepare for future challenges.

E2. Upgrade this theatres/cinemas paragraph (15 min)

Band 6.5 version

Some people say theatres are not important now because people watch everything online. It is cheaper and easier. So theatres and cinemas are useless in digital age.

Tasks

  1. Soften extreme language (useless).
  2. Add precise vocabulary.
  3. Keep it as View A only (not your full opinion yet).
  4. Use one concession-ready ending.

Model Band 7 version — E2

Some people argue that theatres and cinemas have become less necessary in the digital age. Streaming platforms allow audiences to access a huge range of films and performances cheaply and conveniently at home. As a result, traditional venues may seem less essential for everyday entertainment than they were in the past.

E3. Linking + grammar + vocabulary mini-test (15 min)

  1. (Linker) Competition can be motivating; __________, co-operation is more useful at work.
  2. (Grammar) There __________ (be) several reasons why theatres remain important.
  3. (Vocabulary) Replace “work together”: children should learn to __________.
  4. (Complex) Combine: Online viewing is convenient. Live theatre is still valuable. (while)
  5. (Discussion skill) Rewrite so the opinion is clear: Both sides are important.

Answers — E3

  1. however / nevertheless
  2. are
  3. collaborate / co-operate
  4. While online viewing is convenient, live theatre is still valuable.
  5. Possible: Both sides have value, but I believe co-operation / theatres should take priority.
