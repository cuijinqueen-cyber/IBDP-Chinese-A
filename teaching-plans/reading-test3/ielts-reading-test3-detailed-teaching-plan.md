## 2. Master problem-solving toolkit (teach once, reuse always)

### 2.1 Before reading questions deeply
1. Read title → predict topic.  
2. Skim paragraph first sentences → build a **paragraph map**.  
3. Mark text type: *description / chronology / problem-solution / argument*.

### 2.2 Notes / Summary completion (ONE WORD ONLY)
1. Read the note title + subheadings (they show which paragraphs to search).  
2. Predict grammar: noun? plural? uncountable?  
3. Underline keywords around the blank.  
4. Scan passage for paraphrase of those keywords.  
5. Copy **exact word** from passage (no synonyms of your own).  
6. Check: singular/plural and spelling.

### 2.3 TRUE / FALSE / NOT GIVEN
1. Underline absolute / comparison / quantity words (*evenly, most, more than, significant*).  
2. Locate the relevant sentence.  
3. Decide:
   - **TRUE** = same meaning (paraphrase OK)  
   - **FALSE** = passage says the opposite  
   - **NOT GIVEN** = point not addressed (often a missing comparison or extra claim)

### 2.4 YES / NO / NOT GIVEN (Passage 3)
Same logic as T/F/NG, but about **writer’s views**, not just facts.

### 2.5 Multiple choice (writer’s purpose)
1. Read question stem carefully (*What is the writer doing…?*).  
2. Re-read the named paragraph.  
3. Eliminate options that are too narrow, too extreme, or not in that paragraph.

### 2.6 Matching sentence endings
1. Scan for unique anchors (place names, dates, species).  
2. Read the full idea around the anchor.  
3. Match meaning, then confirm grammar of the completed sentence.

# PASSAGE 1 — *Saving the saiga*  
## Detailed text analysis

### 3.1 Text structure (problem–solution chronology)

| Section | Paragraphs (approx.) | Function | Problem-solving value |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Orientation | Opening | What/where saiga are; mostly Kazakhstan | Distribution claims for T/F/NG |
| B Adaptations | Next | Nose + winter coat | Notes Q1–3 |
| C History of decline | Next | Near extinction → protection → post-1991 crash | Timeline traps |
| D Human threats | Next | Poaching (horns); ironic rhino link; habitat barriers | Notes Q4–6; T/F on rhinos |
| E Disease | Next | 2015 & 2017 mass mortality | Notes “disease” bullet |
| F Climate | Next | Extremes, aridity, streams vanish | Notes Q7; comparison traps |
| G Conservation solution | Next | Altyn Dala Initiative + partners; UN flagship | Multi-species purpose |
| H Recovery outcome | Final | From 21,000 → 1.9m+; still critically endangered but improving | Numbers / direction |

**Problem chain in the text**
1. Overhunting / poaching (horns)  
2. Habitat loss + migration barriers  
3. Disease outbreaks  
4. Climate stress  

**Solution chain in the text**
1. Legal protection (historical)  
2. Multi-partner ecosystem restoration (Altyn Dala)  
3. Monitoring via aerial census  
4. Result: strong population rebound (direction positive; status still endangered)

### 3.2 Key paraphrase bank (Passage 1)

| Passage idea | Likely paraphrase in questions |
|---|---|
| bulbous nose filters dust | keep dust out |
| cools blood / warms air | lower temperature of blood; warm air |
| heavy winter coat shed in warm weather | thick coat … loses in spring |
| horns used in traditional medicine | poaching for horns |
| agriculture/settlement | farms and settlements |
| railways/pipelines/fences | barriers / loss of migration routes |
| streams dried up | disappearance of streams |
| many species they support | benefit of a number of different animals |

## 4. Passage 1 questions — detailed solving

### Questions 1–7 (Notes: ONE WORD ONLY)

**Global strategy:** Subheading *Adaptations* → early paragraphs; *Reasons for population decline* → later threat paragraphs. Answers follow passage order.

| Q | Note clue | Locate / paraphrase | Answer | Why students miss it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | keep ___ out | nose filters **dust** | **dust** | write *dirt/sand* |
| 2 | lower temperature of its ___ in summer | cools **blood** | **blood** | write *body* |
| 3 | grows a thick ___ in winter | winter **coat** | **coat** | write *fur/hair* |
| 4 | poaching especially for ___ of male saiga | hunted for **horns** | **horns** | plural needed |
| 5 | reduction in size of saiga’s ___ | habitat loss | **habitat** | write *area/land* |
| 6 | loss of access to the ___ used for migration | migration **routes** | **routes** | write *paths/roads* |
| 7 | climate change → disappearance of ___ | smaller **streams** vanished | **streams** | write *rivers/water* |

**Teaching drill:** For each blank, force prediction (*noun? plural?*) before scanning.

### Questions 8–13 (TRUE / FALSE / NOT GIVEN)

| Q | Statement focus | Evidence logic | Answer | Solver tip |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | numbers **evenly** across four countries | >90% in Kazakhstan; small numbers elsewhere | **FALSE** | “evenly” is the killer word |
| 9 | for **most of 20th century** population **falling** | recovered under protection in 20th c.; crash after 1991 | **FALSE** | check whole century, not one decade |
| 10 | protecting rhinos in Africa affected saiga | conservationists promoted saiga horn as rhino alternative → crash | **TRUE** | causal link is stated |
| 11 | climate fluctuations threaten Central Asia **more than elsewhere** | climate threat mentioned; **no world comparison** | **NOT GIVEN** | comparison = classic NG |
| 12 | Altyn Dala benefits **a number of different animals** | restore ecosystems & many species, including saiga | **TRUE** | “including” = multi-species |
| 13 | UN recognition → **additional international funding** | recognition stated; funding not mentioned | **NOT GIVEN** | extra consequence not in text |

**FALSE vs NOT GIVEN clinic (use Q8 vs Q11):**
- Q8: text gives distribution data that **contradicts** “evenly” → FALSE  
- Q11: text never compares regions → NOT GIVEN

# PASSAGE 2 — *The problems of getting around Dar es Salaam*  
## Detailed text analysis

### 5.1 Text structure (urban problem → chosen solution → remaining problems)

| Block | Content | Problem-solving role |
|---|---|---|
| Growth pressure | Fastest-growing city; huge future population | Context for urgency |
| Housing/commute problems | Informal edge settlements; 2+ hour trips; mud roads | Living conditions claims |
| Transport problems | Congestion; tiny rail; unsafe *boda boda* | Existing system failure |
| Root cause | Old arterial roads for 35,000; informal peripheral growth | Planning failure |
| Strategic choice | Reject private-car/metro path → choose **buses** | Solution selection |
| DART features | Dedicated lanes; pre-pay; step-free access | Notes “Features” |
| User benefit + flaws | Journey time cut; AC off to save fuel | Mixed evaluation |
| Operational problems | Flood → bus shortage; smartcards/scanners fail; queues | Notes “Problems” |
| Expansion plan | Phases II–IV; walk-access goal | Future scale |
| Why BRT not metro | Local stakeholders; local industry involvement | Argument for solution type |

**Problem–solution map (teach explicitly)**

```
PROBLEMS
├─ rapid urban growth
├─ long unsafe/uncomfortable commute
├─ outdated road network
└─ informal sprawl
        ↓
CHOSEN SOLUTION
└─ DART Bus Rapid Transit (cheaper than metro)
        ↓
PARTIAL SUCCESS
└─ faster journeys for users
        ↓
REMAINING PROBLEMS
├─ fuel-saving → weak AC
├─ flood → too few buses
├─ broken smartcard/QR systems
└─ manual ticket checks → queues
        ↓
NEXT STEPS
└─ expand phases + local capacity building
```

### 5.2 Why this passage is perfect for “problem-solving approach” teaching
- It does **not** present a perfect solution.  
- Students learn to separate: *intended design features* vs *current operational failures*.  
- This mirrors Band 7 thinking in Writing Task 2 problem–solution essays too.

## 6. Passage 2 questions — detailed solving

### Questions 14–18 (TRUE / FALSE / NOT GIVEN)

| Q | Critical words | Text check | Answer | Explanation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | rising **more rapidly than previously predicted** | Growth numbers exist; no comparison with earlier predictions | **NOT GIVEN** | prediction comparison missing |
| 15 | **most** live in **high-rise** on the edge | Most live in **single-storey** informal settlements | **FALSE** | housing type contradicted |
| 16 | residents **consulted** about suburban rail | Rail mentioned; consultation not mentioned | **NOT GIVEN** | extra claim |
| 17 | **majority** of present residential development **unplanned** | Expansion informal / without agreed strategy | **TRUE** | paraphrase of informal growth |
| 18 | authorities follow plan of **large number of African cities** | Text criticises metro obsession; Dar chose BRT instead | **FALSE** | they are **not** following the common metro model |

**Micro-lesson:** Quantifiers (*most / majority*) and comparisons (*more than / large number*) decide many answers.

### Questions 19–26 (Notes: Features / Problems)

Order follows text: features first, then problems.

| Q | Clue | Answer | Passage anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 19 | designated ___ to cut delays | **lanes** | separate bus lanes |
| 20 | pay fares before ___ | **boarding** | ticket before boarding |
| 21 | passengers in ___ can use every part | **wheelchairs** | step-free for wheelchairs (and buggies) |
| 22 | AC off to reduce ___ use | **fuel** | save fuel |
| 23 | shortage due to severe ___ | **flood** | flood at depot |
| 24 | unable to use ___ | **smartcards** | smartcard readers not working |
| 25 | checked manually at station ___ | **gates** | staff at gates tear tickets |
| 26 | ___ build up in rush hours | **queues** | queues considerable |

**Common errors**
- Q19: *roads* (too general)  
- Q21: *buggies* (incomplete; note says passengers **in** ___)  
- Q24: *tickets/QR codes* (equipment issue is smartcards/readers)  
- Synonym invention breaks ONE WORD FROM PASSAGE rule

# PASSAGE 3 — *Rethinking the Past*  
## Detailed text analysis

### 7.1 Text structure (academic argument)

| Move | What writer does | Teaching label |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | New discoveries force rethink of human evolution timeline | Hook / claim |
| 2 | Two focus points: earlier “advanced” behaviours; gender-role bias | Thesis roadmap |
| 3 | Architecture evidence (Kalambo Falls logs; Denisovan high-altitude) | Evidence set A |
| 4 | Art evidence (Neanderthals, *H. erectus*, disputed *H. naledi*) | Evidence set B |
| 5 | Controversy over *H. naledi* methods/publicity | Complication |
| 6 | Brain size fixation is misleading; wiring matters | Counter-assumption |
| 7 | Evolution is incremental (mobile-phone analogy) | Conceptual model |
| 8 | Archaeology shaped by patriarchal preconceptions | Bias diagnosis |
| 9 | New data: women hunted widely; female leaders misidentified | Evidence overturning myth |
| 10 | Inequality/patriarchy not inevitable | Writer’s conclusion |

**Problem-solving approach inside the passage (meta-teaching)**
1. **Identify old problem/assumption:** e.g. only *Homo sapiens* did complex work; men were hunters/leaders.  
2. **Bring new evidence:** dated artefacts, meta-analysis, protein analysis.  
3. **Challenge methods/bias:** measurable brain size; patriarchal research lenses.  
4. **Offer revised conclusion:** more complex, less inevitable inequality.

This is ideal for teaching students how academic Reading Passage 3 arguments move.

### 7.2 Difficulty profile
- Harder vocabulary + abstract ideas  
- Questions test **writer purpose** and **attitude**, not only facts  
- Y/N/NG requires separating writer view from mentioned claims of others

## 8. Passage 3 questions — detailed solving

### Questions 27–30 (Multiple choice — writer’s purpose)

| Q | Focus | Best approach | Likely answer direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 27 | What writer is doing in 2nd paragraph | Purpose, not detail | **A** pinpointing key changes in understanding (advanced behaviour earlier; gender bias) |
| 28 | Mobile phones in later paragraph | Analogy function | **A** developments usually gradual/incremental |
| 29 | “unpick this stuff” | Reference of pronoun/phrase | **D** reevaluating research shaped by outdated social beliefs |
| 30 | Final paragraph suggestion | Writer’s implication | **A** studying past may help rethink fairness today / inequality not inevitable |

**Solver routine for purpose MCQs**
1. Ignore attractive detail options.  
2. Ask: *Is the writer describing, arguing, exemplifying, or concluding?*  
3. Match option verb (*pinpointing / outlining / summarising*).

### Questions 31–34 (Matching endings A–F)

Anchors → meaning:

| Q | Anchor | Matching idea | Ending (letter) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 31 | Kalambo Falls | early sophisticated construction before *H. sapiens* | **E** (*H. sapiens* not only species with sophisticated workmanship) |
| 32 | high-altitude regions | other species survived harsh environments earlier | **F** |
| 33 | June 2023 publication | women hunted in most foraging societies | **D** experts mistaken about who looked for food |
| 34 | Iberian leader analysis | previous power assumptions wrong (assumed male) | **B** |

(Options A/C are distractors: A fits opening age-of-species idea but not these anchors; C overclaims extinct-hominin gender vs sapiens.)

### Questions 35–40 (YES / NO / NOT GIVEN — writer’s views)

| Q | Claim | Decision logic | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 35 | Neanderthal paintings were likely the **first** art ever | Text says Neanderthals painted; not that they were first | **NOT GIVEN** |
| 36 | Prehistoric shell artwork is **very rare** | Engraving on shells mentioned; rarity not judged | **NOT GIVEN** |
| 37 | Rising Star methods were rather unconventional | Dispute intensified by non-traditional release/methods | **YES** (aligned with writer’s report of unconventional approach) |
| 38 | Age of Rising Star etchings is unclear | If text doesn’t state dating uncertainty clearly → often NG; if controversy includes dating, check carefully | **NOT GIVEN** *(unless passage explicitly says age uncertain)* |
| 39 | Publicity methods added to controversy | Text links non-traditional release to intensified dispute | **YES** |
| 40 | Brain size is key to whether *H. naledi* could make art | Writer argues brain-size focus is misleading; wiring matters | **NO** (contradicts writer’s view) |

**Teaching caution on 37–38:** always verify against exact wording in the student’s edition; train the process even if one item is debated.

## 10. Lesson procedures (detailed)

### Lesson 1 — Passage 1 map + Notes 1–7 (90 min)
1. Predict from title (5)  
2. Paragraph mapping in pairs (15)  
3. Teach Notes routine (10)  
4. Guided Q1–3 (15)  
5. Independent Q4–7 (20)  
6. Error autopsy: synonym temptation (15)  
7. Homework: rewrite wrong answers with evidence line numbers/phrases

### Lesson 2 — Passage 1 T/F/NG (90 min)
1. Mini-lesson FALSE vs NG with invented examples (15)  
2. Timed Q8–13 (18)  
3. Evidence board: quote + decision (30)  
4. Make 3 student-written trap statements for peers (20)  
5. Reflection checklist (7)

### Lesson 3 — Passage 2 problem–solution map (90 min)
1. Build problem tree on board (20)  
2. Identify solution choice and why not metro (15)  
3. T/F/NG 14–18 timed (15)  
4. Deep review focusing on Q15/Q18 quantifiers (25)  
5. Link to Writing: 5-sentence oral summary of problems/solution (15)

### Lesson 4 — DART notes + evaluating solutions (90 min)
1. Separate “design features” vs “implementation problems” (15)  
2. Notes 19–26 (25)  
3. Discuss: Is DART a success, partial success, or failing? Cite text (25)  
4. Language upgrade: *capacity, designated lanes, stakeholders* (15)  
5. Homework paragraph: evaluate one solution with one remaining problem

### Lesson 5 — Passage 3 argument + MCQ (90 min)
1. Trace argument moves 1–10 (20)  
2. Teach purpose verbs (10)  
3. Q27–30 process talk-aloud (30)  
4. Analogy analysis (mobile phones) (15)  
5. Vocabulary: *preconceptions, incremental, meta-analysis* (15)

### Lesson 6 — Matching + Y/N/NG + synthesis (90 min)
1. Anchor scanning drill for 31–34 (20)  
2. Y/N/NG 35–40 (25)  
3. Full mixed review quiz from all 3 passages (25)  
4. Personal error profile (FC of reading: timing, traps, vocab) (20)
