## Passage 1: Saving the saiga

### Text problem–solution snapshot
- **Problems:** poaching (horns), habitat/migration barriers, disease mass-mortality, climate/aridity  
- **Responses:** historical legal protection → Altyn Dala multi-partner restoration → aerial monitoring  
- **Outcome:** rebound to >1.9m in Kazakhstan; still critically endangered, trend positive

### Notes 1–7 — step cards

**Q1 keep ____ out**  
Predict: noun (something blocked by nose).  
Scan: bulbous nose / filter.  
Answer: **dust**

**Q2 lower temperature of its ____**  
Predict: body-part noun.  
Scan: cools blood in summer.  
Answer: **blood**

**Q3 thick ____ in winter**  
Predict: clothing/covering noun.  
Scan: heavy winter coat shed later.  
Answer: **coat**

**Q4 poaching for ____ of male saiga**  
Predict: body-part plural.  
Scan: horns / traditional medicine.  
Answer: **horns**

**Q5 reduction in size of ____**  
Predict: place/range noun.  
Scan: habitat loss from farms/settlements.  
Answer: **habitat**

**Q6 access to the ____ for migration**  
Predict: pathway noun plural.  
Scan: railways/pipelines/fences block migration routes.  
Answer: **routes**

**Q7 disappearance of ____**  
Predict: water noun plural.  
Scan: streams dried up.  
Answer: **streams**

## Passage 2: Dar es Salaam transport

### Text problem–solution snapshot
- **Problems:** explosive growth, long commutes, congestion, outdated arteries, unsafe informal transport, unplanned sprawl  
- **Chosen solution:** DART BRT (not metro)  
- **Success signal:** journey time cut dramatically for some users  
- **Remaining problems:** AC/fuel, flood-related bus shortage, broken fare tech, queues  
- **Next solution stage:** network phases + local industry/stakeholder model

### T/F/NG 14–18

| Q | Trap word | Answer | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | more rapidly than predicted | NG | No prediction comparison |
| 15 | high-rise | FALSE | Single-storey informal housing |
| 16 | consulted | NG | No consultation info |
| 17 | majority unplanned | TRUE | Informal peripheral growth |
| 18 | follow large number of African cities | FALSE | Rejects metro-obsession model |

### Notes 19–26

| Q | Subhead | Answer | Proof phrase |
|---|---|---|---|
| 19 | Features | lanes | separate/designated bus lanes |
| 20 | Features | boarding | pay before boarding |
| 21 | Features | wheelchairs | step-free access |
| 22 | Problems | fuel | AC off to save fuel |
| 23 | Problems | flood | flood at depot |
| 24 | Problems | smartcards | readers not working |
| 25 | Problems | gates | staff at gates |
| 26 | Problems | queues | queues at peak times |

### Evaluation question for class discussion
> Is DART presented as a complete solution or a partial solution?  
> Cite one success + two unresolved problems.

## Passage 3: Rethinking the Past

### Argument problem–solving snapshot
- **Intellectual problem:** old, biased, incomplete models of prehistory  
- **Data:** earlier architecture/art; women hunting; mis-sexed leaders  
- **Method problem:** overfocus on brain size; patriarchal assumptions  
- **Revised claim:** sophisticated behaviour older/wider; inequality not inevitable

### MCQ 27–30 process

**Q27 (2nd paragraph purpose)**  
Ask: Does paragraph announce the writer’s two big shifts?  
→ **A**

**Q28 (mobile phones)**  
Ask: What point does the analogy serve?  
→ gradual/incremental change = **A**

**Q29 (unpick this stuff)**  
Ask: Unpick what? Old patriarchal-influenced conclusions.  
→ **D**

**Q30 (final paragraph)**  
Ask: What does writer want reader to take away?  
→ past shows alternatives; inequality not inevitable → **A**
