2. Why 6.5 Students Usually Stall (and What Band 7 Needs)

A typical 6.5 writer already produces a complete essay with a clear position. The jump to 7/7.5 usually fails in one or more of these areas:

CriterionTypical 6.5 patternBand 7–7.5 target
Task Response / AchievementPosition is clear, but ideas stay general; “to what extent” is ignoredFully addresses all parts; ideas are extended with precise support
Coherence & CohesionParagraphs exist, but links feel mechanical (Firstly, Moreover)Clear central topic in each paragraph; natural progression
Lexical ResourceSafe vocabulary; some repetition / awkward collocationTopic vocabulary + less common items used accurately
GrammarMix of simple/complex; frequent small errorsFrequent error-free complex sentences; strong control of tense/articles/clauses

Teaching priority order for this student:

  1. Idea development & precision (Task Response)
  2. Paragraph logic (CC)
  3. Grammar accuracy under exam pressure (GRA)
  4. Vocabulary upgrade without forcing rare words (LR)

4. Detailed Lesson Plans

Lesson 1 — Diagnostic & Target Setting (90 min)

Aim: Establish exact band profile and create a personal improvement map.

Materials: Cambridge Writing Task 1 (cities graph) + Task 2 (science aim).

Procedure

  1. Warm-up (10 min)

Ask: What do you think “Band 7 writing” looks like? Collect beliefs about word count, big vocabulary, and complex grammar. Correct myths early.

  1. Timed diagnostic (60 min)
  • Task 1: 20 minutes (cities line graph)
  • Task 2: 40 minutes (science aim — agree/disagree)

Exam conditions: no dictionary, handwritten or typed as per student’s real test format.

  1. Immediate self-check (10 min)

Student answers:

  • Did I fully answer “to what extent”?
  • Did I select key features or list every number?
  • Which paragraph felt weakest?
  1. Teacher marking after class

Score all 4 criteria for both tasks. Annotate only high-impact issues (max 6–8 comments total).

Homework

  • Student rewrites only the introduction + one body paragraph of Task 2 after reading teacher comments.
  • Keep an “Error Log” notebook with 3 columns: Error → Correct form → My rule.

Exit outcome

Personal target sheet, e.g.:

  • Task 2: extend ideas beyond general statements
  • Task 1: overview must include comparison + trend
  • Grammar: reduce article and subject–verb errors

Lesson 2 — Agree/Disagree Essay Architecture (90 min)

Aim: Install a reliable Band-7 structure for “To what extent do you agree or disagree?”

Materials: Opinion essay mini-lesson (space programme vs vital public services).

Core teaching point from the material

Use the material’s planning sequence, upgraded for Band 7:

  1. Read & highlight key parts
  2. Plan essay structure
  3. Plan ideas for each main paragraph
  4. Write with a clear position from the first sentence of the introduction

Recommended Band-7 structure (teach explicitly)

ParagraphJobMini-formula
IntroductionTopic paraphrase + clear extent of agreementWhile X has some value, I largely agree that…
Body 1Strongest reason supporting positionClaim → Explain → Example → Link back
Body 2Second reason or limited concession + rebuttalSame development chain
ConclusionRestate position + summarise logicNo new ideas

Procedure

  1. Question decoding (15 min)

Prompt from material:

The money spent by governments on space programmes would be better spent on vital public services, such as schools and hospitals. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Highlight: government money / space programmes / better spent / vital public services / to what extent.

  1. Structure modelling (20 min)

Build the material’s outline into a cleaner Band-7 plan:

  • Intro: government spending debate → largely agree: public services should take priority
  • BP1: space spending often delivers limited immediate social return / high cost & failure risk
  • BP2: public services create broad, measurable benefits (education, health, safety)
  • Conclusion: prioritise services that benefit society widely
  1. Idea quality upgrade (25 min)

Take the material’s raw ideas and train “6.5 → 7” development:

6.5 idea (from material)Band 7 upgrade
waste of money when projects failfailed missions absorb large public budgets with little transferable benefit to citizens
no benefit to normal peopleordinary taxpayers rarely experience direct gains compared with local hospitals/schools
cheaper e.g. doctors/teachersinvesting in essential workers yields higher social return per unit of spending
reduce poverty / better quality of lifeaccessible education and healthcare improve long-term productivity and life expectancy
  1. Guided paragraph writing (25 min)

Student writes Body Paragraph 1 only (12–14 minutes), then teacher hot-corrects for:

  • one clear topic sentence
  • explanation (not just listing)
  • one specific example
  • ending that supports the overall position
  1. Wrap-up (5 min)

Student states their personal template in one sentence.

In-class exercises (15–20 min, can replace part of guided writing if needed)

  • A1 Sort the linkers
  • A3 Replace mechanical linkers (Firstly / Moreover / In conclusion)
  • A4 Build one cohesive body paragraph with 4 linkers

Homework

Write a full 250+ word essay on the space vs public services question in 40 minutes.

Plus: A2 linker choice exercise.

Self-check checklist:

  • Position clear in introduction
  • Each body paragraph has one central idea
  • Example is specific
  • Conclusion matches introduction position
  • No sentence-start linker in every sentence

Lesson 3 — Task 2 Deep Dive: Science Essay (90 min)

Aim: Transfer the structure from Lesson 2 to the diagnostic Task 2 prompt and raise Task Response to Band 7+.

Materials:

“The most important aim of science should be to improve people’s lives. To what extent do you agree or disagree?”

Key teaching challenge

Many 6.5 answers say “I agree” and give two general benefits of science. Band 7 answers address “the most important aim” and “to what extent”.

Strong position options to teach

  • Largely agree: improving lives should be the primary aim; other aims (curiosity, defence, prestige) are secondary.
  • Partly agree: improving lives is vital, but pure research also matters because it later enables life-improving breakthroughs.
  • Avoid a vague “I agree and disagree equally” with no hierarchy.

Procedure

  1. Feedback on Lesson 2 homework (15 min)

Mark for TR/CC first. Ignore minor spelling unless it blocks meaning.

  1. Position clinic (15 min)

Student chooses one clear stance and writes a 2-sentence introduction only. Compare weak vs strong:

  • Weak: I agree that science should improve people’s lives.
  • Strong: I largely agree that the principal purpose of scientific work should be to enhance human wellbeing, even though curiosity-driven research can play a supporting role.
  1. Planning board (20 min)

Plan together:

If largely agree

  • BP1: science funded by society has an ethical duty to reduce suffering (medicine, clean energy, food security)
  • BP2: when science prioritises prestige or profit alone, social needs are neglected; therefore life improvement should remain the main aim
  • Optional concession: basic research is valuable, but still justified by long-term human benefit
  1. Timed body writing (30 min)

Student writes both body paragraphs (25 min). Teacher monitors for:

  • over-general claims (Science is very important)
  • missing explanation after examples
  • paragraph drift
  1. Micro conclusion drill (10 min)

Write conclusion in 4 minutes. Criteria: mirrors position; no new argument; 2–3 sentences.

In-class exercises (10–15 min)

  • B5 Science gap-fill paragraph (activates target lexis before writing)
  • A2 review if linker use was mechanical in Lesson 2 homework

Homework

Complete full essay (intro already drafted in class). Then rewrite one body paragraph using the Claim → Explain → Example → Link chain.

Plus: B2 vocabulary upgrade + B4 write 8 original phrase-bank sentences.

Lesson 4 — Lexis & Grammar Lift to Band 7 (90 min)

Aim: Reduce recurring accuracy errors and upgrade topic vocabulary without sounding unnatural.

Materials: Student’s science essay + space essay.

Procedure

  1. Error Log review (20 min)

From the student’s last two essays, extract top 8 repeated issues. Typical 6.5 cluster:

  • articles (the science, improve life of people)
  • countable/uncountable (researches, informations)
  • tense consistency in examples
  • weak reference (this / it / which)
  • sentence fragments after Although / Because
  1. Precision vocabulary upgrade (25 min)

Build a personal phrase bank from both essay topics:

TopicBand 6.5 wordingBand 7 alternatives
Science aimsmake life betterenhance public wellbeing; alleviate suffering; raise living standards
Research valuefind new thingsadvance fundamental knowledge; yield long-term applications
Government spendingwaste moneymisallocate public funds; opportunity cost; social return on investment
Public serviceshelp peopledeliver essential services; reduce inequality; strengthen social infrastructure

Rule: student may use a new phrase only if they can write one original sentence with it correctly.

  1. Complex sentence control (25 min)

Practice 3 high-value patterns only (quality > quantity):

  • Concession: Although space exploration can generate technological spin-offs, …
  • Cause–effect: If governments underfund healthcare, …
  • Relative clause: Research that prioritises human needs …

Drill: convert 6 simple sentences from the student’s essay into accurate complex ones.

  1. Targeted rewrite (20 min)

Student rewrites the weakest paragraph from the science essay using:

  • 2 upgraded collocations
  • 1 concession sentence
  • zero article errors

In-class exercises (use inside stages above)

  • B1 collocation match
  • B3 word forms
  • D1 articles & countable nouns
  • D2 complex sentence combining
  • D3 error correction paragraph
  • Finish with E1 paragraph upgrade if time allows

Homework

  • D4 conditionals & concession completions
  • D6 relative clause upgrade
  • Add 10 personal collocations to phrase bank (science / education / health / government)
  • Reuse 5 new items in a 4-sentence mini-paragraph

Lesson 5 — Task 1 Essentials: Line Graph Control (90 min)

Aim: Produce a clear Band-7 Task 1 with overview, selection of key features, and accurate comparisons.

Materials: Cities line graph (Philippines, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia; 1970–2040).

What Band 7 Task 1 requires

  • Clear overview (not a detail dump)
  • Grouping and comparison
  • Accurate data language
  • Predictions handled cautiously (is projected to, is expected to)

Recommended Task 1 structure

  1. Introduction — paraphrase the question
  2. Overview — 2–3 major trends
  3. Detail 1 — highest / most dynamic lines (e.g. Malaysia + Indonesia)
  4. Detail 2 — remaining countries / contrasts (Philippines fluctuations + Thailand slower rise)

Procedure

  1. Graph reading protocol (20 min)

Train a fixed 4-step scan:

  • What is measured? (% of population in cities)
  • Time span? (1970–2040, including forecasts)
  • Biggest pattern? (overall urbanisation rises in all four countries)
  • Stand-out contrasts? (Malaysia sharp rise; Philippines dip then recovery; Thailand lowest for long periods; Indonesia strong climb)
  1. Overview workshop (20 min)

Write 3 overview versions; pick the best.

Model target:

Overall, the proportion of people living in cities is projected to rise in all four countries. Malaysia is expected to remain the most urbanised by 2040, while Thailand, despite growth, is likely to stay comparatively lower for much of the period.

  1. Comparison language bank (20 min)

Practise:

  • overtook / remained below / rose steadily / fluctuated / is predicted to reach
  • Avoid listing every decade for every country.
  1. Timed detail paragraphs (25 min)

Student writes Body 1 + Body 2 in 20 minutes using selected data only.

  1. Quick accuracy check (5 min)

Circle every number/date; verify against graph.

In-class exercises

  • C1 trend verb choice
  • C2 paraphrase the question (3 versions)
  • C4 overview gap-fill
  • A5 Task 1 comparison linkers

Homework

Write full Task 1 (20 minutes). Then write a second overview only (5 minutes) without looking at the first.

Plus: C3 data language upgrade.

Lesson 6 — Task 1 Precision & Band 7 Polish (90 min)

Aim: Eliminate common Task 1 ceiling errors that keep candidates at 6.5.

Materials: Same urbanisation graph + student’s Lesson 5 script.

High-frequency 6.5 Task 1 problems to attack

  • No real overview (or overview mixed into details)
  • Mechanical chronology with no grouping
  • Inaccurate data (about 30% when it is closer to 20%)
  • Repetitive verbs (increase / increase / increase)
  • Opinion language (I think this is good)

Procedure

  1. Feedback conference (20 min)

Score student’s Task 1 by criterion. Show one Band-7 sample paragraph (teacher-written from the same graph).

  1. Data selection game (15 min)

From the graph, student may choose only 8 data points total for the whole answer. Forces selection over listing.

  1. Language upgrade (25 min)

Transform repetitive sentences:

  • Malaysia increased… Indonesia increased… Thailand increased…

→ Malaysia rose sharply… Indonesia climbed more gradually… Thailand remained relatively stable before accelerating…

  1. Full rewrite under light time pressure (25 min)

Rewrite complete Task 1 in 18–20 minutes.

  1. Peer-style checklist (5 min)

Student marks own script:

  • Paraphrased intro
  • Overview with at least two big features
  • Comparisons present
  • Future language used for 2030/2040
  • No opinions

In-class exercises

  • C5 avoid repetition of increase
  • D5 Task 1 tense control
  • E2 Task 1 paragraph upgrade

Homework

One new line-graph Task 1 from a different topic (teacher provides). Apply the same 4-paragraph template.

Plus: review any missed grammar items from D1–D3.

Lesson 7 — Integrated Skills & Transfer (90 min)

Aim: Prove the student can transfer strategies to unseen prompts, not only the two study texts.

Materials:

  • Strategy sheet from Lessons 2–6
  • One new agree/disagree prompt (teacher-selected, similar difficulty)
  • One new Task 1 visual (bar/line preferred)

Procedure

  1. Strategy retrieval (10 min)

Student explains, without notes:

  • Task 2 planning steps
  • Task 1 overview formula
  • Personal top 3 grammar risks
  1. Split timed practice (60 min)
  • Task 1: 20 min
  • Task 2: 40 min

Same exam rules as Lesson 1.

  1. Comparative debrief (20 min)

Compare with Lesson 1 diagnostic:

  • clearer position?
  • stronger paragraph focus?
  • better overview?
  • fewer repeated grammar faults?

In-class / homework exercises

  • E3 mixed mini-test (linking + grammar + vocabulary) before or after the timed practice
  • Personal weak-area pack: teacher selects 1 section from A/B/C/D based on Lesson 7 mistakes

Homework

Revise only the weaker task from today’s practice (full rewrite). Prepare questions for final mock.

Continue Section F daily 10-minute drills until the test.

5. Using the Two Source Materials Across the Course

A) Opinion mini-lesson (space vs public services)

Use as the training wheels for Task 2:

  • Lesson 2: structure + idea planning (as written in the material)
  • Then upgrade the material’s bullet ideas into fully developed Band-7 paragraphs
  • Keep the material’s useful habits: highlight key words → plan structure → plan paragraph ideas before writing

Important upgrade from the material for Band 7

The material’s outline (“stop space spending” / “public services are better”) is clear, but for “to what extent” answers, teach a more precise stance, e.g.:

  • I largely agree that essential public services should take priority over space programmes.

This sounds more Band 7 than absolute “space spending should be stopped,” unless the student can justify a hardline view carefully.

B) Cambridge Task 1 graph + Task 2 science question

Use as the spine of assessment:

  • Lesson 1 diagnostic
  • Lessons 3–6 skill building on the same authentic prompts
  • Measure progress by rewriting the same tasks to a higher standard before moving to unseen mocks

7. Band 7 Writing Targets — Student Checklist

Before every Task 2

  • I answered to what extent, not only yes/no
  • My position is in the introduction and stays consistent
  • Each body paragraph has one main idea
  • Every idea is explained, not only listed
  • I used one precise example
  • Conclusion matches my position

Before every Task 1

  • Introduction is paraphrased
  • Overview describes big trends/comparisons
  • I grouped data instead of listing everything
  • Comparisons are clear
  • Future years use prediction language
  • No personal opinion

Language control

  • I checked articles and countable nouns
  • I used 2–3 complex sentences accurately
  • I avoided repeating the same verb/adjective
  • I used 3–5 topic phrases from my phrase bank

8. Suggested Model Answers Direction (Teacher Reference)

Task 2 (Science) — target features

  • Clear “largely agree” or “partly agree with hierarchy”
  • Body 1: ethical/social purpose of science (health, environment, living standards)
  • Body 2: why other aims should remain secondary; short concession on pure research
  • Lexis: overarching objective, tangible benefits, curiosity-driven research, long-term applications
  • Grammar: concession + relative clauses with high accuracy

Task 1 (Cities graph) — target features

  • Overview: all rise; Malaysia highest by 2040; Thailand comparatively lower; Indonesia strong growth; Philippines non-linear path
  • Selective data: 1970 starting points, key crossing points, 2040 endings
  • Verbs: rose sharply, overtook, levelled off, is projected to reach, fluctuated
  • No explanation of social causes (not required in Task 1)

Task 2 transfer essay (Space vs public services) — target features

  • Position: public services should generally take priority
  • BP1: high cost / limited immediate public benefit / opportunity cost
  • BP2: broad social returns from schools, hospitals, policing, infrastructure
  • Example use: one concrete reference is enough (e.g. costly failed missions vs under-resourced hospitals), not a history essay

11. Teacher Script: First 3 Minutes of Lesson 2 (Example)

“Today we are not writing a full exam essay yet. We are installing your Band 7 system for agree/disagree questions.

You will highlight the question, decide to what extent, plan two paragraphs, and write one high-quality body paragraph.

The space-programme question is our training question. Later we will use the same system on the science question from your practice test.”

12. Final Notes for the Tutor

  1. Do not chase fancy vocabulary first. At 6.5, unclear development and repeated grammar faults cost more band score than “simple” wording.
  2. Reuse the same two authentic tasks (cities graph + science essay) for measurable before/after gains.
  3. Use the opinion mini-lesson as process training, then demand richer explanation than the bullet list provides.
  4. Mark less, mark better: every script gets a band profile + top 3 actions only.
  5. Aim for stability, not one lucky essay. Band 7.5 usually appears when Band 7 habits are automatic under time pressure.
  6. Exercises must transfer into essays. After every drill set, require 3–5 reused items in the next piece of writing.
