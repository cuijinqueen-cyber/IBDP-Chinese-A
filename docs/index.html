IELTS True/False/Not Given — Evidence-Sentence Teaching Plan

Source: 雅思阅读真经 5 · Chapter 2

Passages: 25 Self-esteem Myth (YES/NO/NG) · 26 William Gilbert and Magnetism (T/F/NG)

Student aim: Band 6.5 → 7.0–7.5

Core skill: Locate the exact evidence sentence, then decide YES/TRUE · NO/FALSE · NOT GIVEN with a fixed routine

0. Teaching principle

For every statement, students must produce a 3-line evidence card:

  1. 1. Locate — quote / paraphrase the sentence(s) that talk about the same topic
  2. 2. Compare — match keywords: same meaning? opposite? missing piece?
  3. 3. Decide — YES/TRUE · NO/FALSE · NOT GIVEN + trap type

Never answer from memory of the “topic.” Always answer from the sentence.

1. Master solving routine (teach once)

StepActionWhy
1Underline subject + verb + extreme/compare words in the statementThese decide the answer
2Scan for names / dates / unique nounsFast locate
3Read 1–2 sentences before and after the hitAvoid half-sentence traps
4Ask three questionsSame? Opposite? Not addressed?
5Label trap typeperson swap / causality / comparison / extra claim / wrong method word

YES/TRUE vs NO/FALSE vs NOT GIVEN (clinic)

DecisionMeaningExample trap
YES / TRUEPassage supports the statement (paraphrase OK)“closely tied” ≈ positive correlation
NO / FALSEPassage says the opposite or a clearly conflicting factChinese invented compass ≠ Arabs invented
NOT GIVENTopic appears, but the specific claim is missingTwo studies mentioned; no happiness comparison between groups

3. Passage 25 — Question-by-question (evidence + analysis)

Task wording in the pack: YES / NO / NOT GIVEN (writer’s claims).

Q1

Statement: Governor claimed that higher self-esteem would benefit government’s revenue.

Evidence sentence(s):

Prodded by State Assemblyman John Vasconcellos, Governor George Deukmejian set up a task force… Vasconcellos argued that raising self-esteem would reduce… At one point, he even expressed the hope that these efforts would one day help balance the state budget… people with high self-regard earn more… and thus pay more in taxes.

Compare:

  • Budget / tax benefit is in the text.
  • But the claim-maker is Vasconcellos, not the Governor.
  • Governor only set up the task force.

Answer: NO

Trap type: person / role swap (Assemblyman ≠ Governor)

Teach: Always check who claimed what. Same idea + wrong person = NO.

Q2

Statement: The report asserted that many social problems stemmed from the low self-esteem of people.

Evidence sentence(s):

…a 1989 volume entitled *The Social Importance of Self-Esteem*, which stated that “many, if not most, of the major problems plaguing society have roots in the low self-esteem of many of the people who make up society.” In reality, the report contained little to support that assertion.

Compare:

  • Statement = what the report asserted → matches the quoted claim.
  • Writer’s later criticism (“little to support”) does not cancel what the report said.

Answer: YES

Trap type: writer’s doubt distractor

Teach: If the statement is about what a report/person claimed, match that claim—even if the writer disagrees with the evidence quality.

Q3

Statement: Good looking is a reliable attribute to measure self-esteem.

Evidence sentence(s):

…similar self-ratings of other attributes often prove to be way off. Consider… self-esteem and physical attractiveness… those who score highly on self-esteem scales… also boast of being physically attractive.

Compare:

  • Text treats attractiveness/self-ratings as unreliable / circular, not a reliable measuring tool.
  • Statement says “reliable attribute to measure” → contradicts writer.

Answer: NO

Trap type: reliability overclaim

Teach: Presence of “attractiveness” in the paragraph ≠ endorsement of it as a measure.

Q4

Statement: High self-esteem helps students achieve better scores.

Evidence sentence(s):

Early work showed positive correlations… Modern efforts have, however, cast doubt on the idea that higher self-esteem actually induces students to do better.

…high self-esteem… fails to improve academic or job performance.

Compare:

  • Early correlation ≠ “helps / induces.”
  • Writer’s current position: does not improve academic performance.

Answer: NO

Trap type: correlation vs causation / outdated finding

Teach: Watch “helps / induces / causes.” Early positive correlation is a classic FALSE bait.

Q5

Statement: Students with better academic performance are likely to achieve later occupational success.

Evidence sentence(s):

Even if raising self-esteem does not foster academic progress, might it serve some purpose later, say, on the job? … links between workers’ self-regard and job performance…

Compare:

  • Text links self-esteem ↔ job, and self-esteem ↔ school.
  • It does not say good students → later job success.

Answer: NOT GIVEN

Trap type: topic neighbour / wrong pair

Teach: Same paragraph can discuss A–B and A–C without ever stating B–C.

Q6

Statement: People with high self-regard are less likely to smoke.

Evidence sentence(s):

…high self-esteem does not lessen a tendency toward violence, that it does not deter adolescents from turning to alcohol, tobacco, drugs and sex…

Compare:

  • “Less likely to smoke” = high SE deters tobacco use.
  • Text: it does not deter tobacco.

Answer: NO

Trap type: negation miss

Teach: Circle “does not / fails to / little to indicate.”

Q7

Statement: There is positive correlation between happiness and self-esteem.

Evidence sentence(s):

…people with high self-esteem are significantly happier than others. They are also less likely to be depressed.

…happiness and self-esteem proved to be closely tied.

Compare:

  • “Significantly happier / closely tied” = positive association / correlation.
  • (Causation is later questioned—but correlation is affirmed.)

Answer: YES

Trap type: none (straight paraphrase)

Teach: Separate “correlated” (YES) from “leads to / causes” (often NG until proven).

Q8

Statement: Adults generally feel much happier than college students do.

Evidence sentence(s):

…University of Utah… more than 13,000 college students… high self-esteem… strongest factor in overall life satisfaction.

Robin Di Matteo… more than 600 adults… Once again, happiness and self-esteem proved to be closely tied.

Compare:

  • Two groups are studied separately.
  • No comparison of adults’ vs students’ happiness levels.

Answer: NOT GIVEN

Trap type: false comparison

Teach: Two parallel findings ≠ a ranking between groups.

Q9

Statement: Low self-esteem may cause ill-health.

Evidence sentence(s):

Also, a poor self-image is a risk factor for certain eating disorders, especially bulimia.

Compare:

  • Writer accepts poor self-image as a risk factor for an illness (bulimia).
  • “May cause ill-health” is a cautious paraphrase of that claim.

Answer: YES

Trap type: narrow example ↔ general wording

Teach: Specific illness example can support a soft general health claim if the writer endorses the link. Still train students to check how strong the wording is.

Q10

Statement: People’s self-esteem can be boosted by taking a training course.

Evidence sentence(s):

Should parents, teachers and therapists seek to boost self-esteem…? …little to indicate that indiscriminately promoting self-esteem… offers society any compensatory benefits…

Compare:

  • Boosting SE is discussed in principle.
  • Training course is never mentioned.

Answer: NOT GIVEN

Trap type: extra method / invented detail

Teach: “Boost” in text + new delivery method in statement = NG.

4. Passage 26 — Question-by-question (evidence + analysis)

Task wording: TRUE / FALSE / NOT GIVEN (information).

Q1

Statement: It was Gilbert who first discovered some substances with magnetic properties.

Evidence sentence(s):

Prior to him, all that was known… was what the ancients knew, that the lodestone possessed magnetic properties and that amber and jet… would attract…

Compare: Magnetic substances were known before Gilbert.

Answer: FALSE

Trap type: “first / pioneer” overclaim

Teach: “Father of the science” ≠ “first discoverer of the phenomenon.”

Q2

Statement: Arabs invented the magnetic compass in which an iron magnet always pointed south.

Evidence sentence(s):

Around the year 1000 they [the early Chinese] discovered that… it always pointed south. From this developed the magnetic compass, which quickly spread to the Arabs and from them to Europe.

Compare:

  • Pointing south: Chinese discovery.
  • Arabs: receivers/transmitters, not inventors.

Answer: FALSE

Trap type: agent swap in a transmission chain

Teach: Follow invent → spread → receive.

Q3

Statement: Odyssey noted that people believed the magnetic mountain would wreck the ship.

Evidence sentence(s):

…or was there a magnetic mountain at the pole, as described in Odyssey, which ships should never approach, because the sailors thought its pull would yank out all their iron nails and fittings.

Compare:

  • Odyssey describes the mountain.
  • Sailors believed it would rip out iron → wreck the ship (same idea).

Answer: TRUE

Trap type: paraphrase of consequence

Teach: “Yank out nails/fittings” ≈ destroy/wreck the ship functionally.

Q4

Statement: Gilbert explained the phenomenon of the magnetic compass in his book De Magnete.

Evidence sentence(s):

In 1600 Gilbert published *De Magnete*… From his experiments, he concluded that the Earth was itself magnetic and that this was the reason compasses pointed north.

Compare: Direct match: book + explanation of compass.

Answer: TRUE

Trap type: none

Teach: Name of book + “reason compasses pointed north” = explained the phenomenon.

Q5

Statement: Gilbert’s mistaken notion about the distinction between electricity and magnetism held back the development of science.

Evidence sentence(s):

Gilbert strongly argued that electricity and magnetism was not the same thing. For evidence, he (incorrectly) pointed out… By keeping clarity, Gilbert’s strong distinction advanced science for nearly 250 years.

Compare:

  • Yes, the distinction was partly mistaken.
  • But it advanced, not held back, science.

Answer: FALSE

Trap type: half-true + wrong result

Teach: Correct premise + wrong conclusion in the statement = FALSE.

Q6

Statement: James Clerk Maxwell demonstrated that there was close relationship between electricity and magnetism.

Evidence sentence(s):

It took James Clerk Maxwell to show electromagnetism is, in fact, two sides of the same coin.

Compare: “Two sides of the same coin” = close relationship / unity.

Answer: TRUE

Trap type: none

Q7

Statement: Gilbert speculated that the moon orbited the Earth by magnetic force.

Evidence sentence(s):

He speculated that the moon might also be a magnet caused to orbit by its magnetic attraction to the Earth.

Compare: Almost word-for-word.

Answer: TRUE

Trap type: none

Q8

Statement: Copernicans such as Galileo favoured traditional cosmology which held that the Earth was the centre of the universe.

Evidence sentence(s):

In traditional cosmology the Earth was fixed… Since the Copernican cosmology needed a new physics… Copernicans such as Johannes Kepler and Galileo were very interested in Gilbert’s magnetic researches.

Compare:

  • Copernicans oppose traditional Earth-centred cosmology.
  • Statement says they favoured traditional cosmology → opposite.

Answer: FALSE

Trap type: camp / school mix-up

Teach: Name “Copernican” already signals anti-traditional stance.

Q9

Statement: Gilbert’s magnetic theories contradicted the traditional cosmology.

Evidence sentence(s):

…just as the Earth spins on its axis in 24 hours.

Gilbert did not, however, express an opinion as to whether this rotating Earth was at the centre… or in orbit around the Sun.

In traditional cosmology the Earth was fixed…

Compare (careful):

  • Traditional: Earth fixed.
  • Gilbert: Earth spins → this does conflict with traditional fixed-Earth cosmology.
  • He stays silent only on centre vs heliocentric — that silence does not cancel the contradiction about rotation.

Answer: TRUE

Trap type: over-narrow reading of “no opinion”

Teach: “No opinion on X” does not erase a clear contradiction on Y already stated.

Q10

Statement: As a scientist, Gilbert set himself apart by favouring an intuitive approach and experiments rather than the deductive reason.

Evidence sentence(s):

Unlike most medieval thinkers, Gilbert was willing to rely on sense experience and his own observations and experiments rather than the authoritative opinion or deductive philosophy of others.

Compare:

  • “Experiments rather than deductive reason” ≈ text.
  • But text says sense experience / observations, not “intuitive approach.”
  • “Intuitive” changes the method claim → conflicts with the precise description.

Answer: FALSE

Trap type: near-synonym that isn’t (intuitive ≠ empirical observation)

Teach: Band 7+ kills answers that replace observation/experiment with intuition.
